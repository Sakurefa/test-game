2. THE AK ENGINEERING PRINCIPLE
-------------------------------

7. CONFIRMED POPULATION SIMULATION MODEL
----------------------------------------
- Population is aggregate/group-based, not continuously simulated person by person.
- Group attributes initially store aggregate values, averages and proportions, not a full joint distribution.
- Effects and date/condition triggers are separate.
- Needs settle at group level.
- Governing distinction: groups are simulation entities; individuals are narrative entities.
- Named characters may exist for events or unique roles, but must not create individual household/economic simulation or be double-counted in population.
- Dimensions such as class, profession, wealth, species, subspecies and capability are independently extensible and Mod-defined.
- Never infer intersection ratios by multiplying independent marginal proportions; avoid automatic full cross-products.
- Effects use explicit source, target, operation and value where applicable. Temporary effects use separate triggers; process due triggers rather than rescanning all historical effects.
- Current approved Mod Effects are immediate state mutations, not active/source-tracked/reversible modifier instances. A later Trigger expiry cannot safely undo a clamped delta or distinguish overlapping changes. The agreed Trigger-driven expiry model therefore remains a future lifecycle mechanism, not a capability already provided by the current Effect whitelist.
- Do not add personal debt, personal wage ledgers, personal purchases or per-person need settlement to ordinary population runtime.

8. CURRENT ENGINE FOUNDATION GUARDRAIL
---------------------------------------
Continue building Engine capabilities, not a concrete population/economy gameplay feature. The generic aggregate population-group runtime and Mod INI initialization contract are implemented; world save schema is 9.

Population group counts are the authoritative runtime population records. A colony's total population is the sum of all its group counts. The scalar `population` field remains only as a derived compatibility/read projection: Engine initialization and every population-group mutation calculate it from the groups, and save/day validation checks that it matches. No runtime operation may use that scalar to reconstruct, apportion or otherwise define group counts. Each group has a stable ID, population count and independent statistical dimensions. Each `dimension.<id>` is a category-to-proportion mapping whose proportions total 1. Dimensions remain independent marginals; never infer joint ratios by multiplying them or generate a Cartesian product.

Mods may define the complete initial group population through registered `population_group` content sections with `colony_id`, `population` and optional `dimension.<id>` fields. When any enabled Mod defines groups for a colony, those individual group counts compose the entire starting population and their sum defines the runtime total. Do not append an automatic `unclassified` remainder, and do not require the sum to equal the colony definition's fallback `population` value. Only when no Mod defines any group does the Engine create one neutral `unclassified` group from that fallback value. Profession and wealth dimensions resolve against their registered profession / wealth_tier Catalog; other dimensions use generic stable IDs.

The existing `colony.population.delta` effect remains compatible: positive deltas enter `unclassified`; negative deltas reduce existing groups proportionally; the total is recalculated from the resulting group counts and may not become negative. Generic Mod Effects may split a group, merge groups or replace one group's dimension distribution without changing colony population; split/merge preserve the total. Schema 1–7 saves migrate by converting the old scalar population once into a neutral group; schema 8 saves retain their group data and gain an empty Trigger Queue during schema-9 migration. After migration, group counts are authoritative. An empty population-group catalog is omitted from the content fingerprint so unchanged schema-8 saves retain compatibility; actual Mod-defined groups participate in the normal content fingerprint. No actual demographic composition, wage, need basket or market rule has been added to Core.

9. PERSONNEL ENTITIES AND SETTLEMENT-DEPENDENT BUILDING EFFECTS
--------------------------------------------------------------
- Named personnel originate from events. Ordinary hiring may abstract selectable candidates from population groups; special events may create predefined/generated people.
- A separate personnel pool stores acquired people and only the identity, role, capabilities, relationships and affiliations they need.
- Personnel may have class/species/organization affiliation or serve independently. They are not a second population ledger.
- If a person is abstracted from a group, hiring/creation rules must explicitly define whether and when the source group count changes. Never double-count.
- Building conditions use actual employed workforce groups and headcounts, not volatile colony averages or unsupported marginal intersections.
- At configured settlement boundaries, inspect only workforce attributes relevant to active conditions; calculate shares from actual employed headcounts; apply/remove persistent effects; keep effects stable until the next scheduled evaluation.
- Coincident recurring simulation-system settlement priority is annual → monthly → weekly → daily. Recurring work belongs to the simulation-system cadence registry; annual/monthly cadence is now supported there, while individual one-shot actions belong to trigger instances.
- Persisted Trigger instances are distinct from recurring system cadence. The current Engine supports one-shot date, whole-day elapsed-time and Domain-Event due sources. A Domain Event due descriptor matches a newly appended fact by exact event type and optional exact scope/subject ID; no history scan, backfill or payload expression is used. The source fact snapshot is stored on the Trigger and its receipt, and activation schedules it for the next completed campaign day. It scans only the bounded pending Trigger Queue (maximum 4096); Event Bus remains post-commit and read-only. Condition/threshold sources remain future work. Trigger sources must not create a second recurring scheduler or duplicate calendar rules.
- Initial due-trigger processing is aligned to completed campaign-day settlement boundaries. `clock.dayFraction` tracks partial-day progress, but does not currently execute a sub-day simulation settlement; sub-day triggers require an explicit future simulation-phase contract.
- Trigger actions, queue removal and recent receipts occur on the detached candidate world during day settlement. The action and queue change commit together only if the day succeeds; a failure discards the candidate. Domain Events are published externally only after the world commit.
- Triggers may invoke only explicitly registered and validated Engine resolutions, never arbitrary Mod functions or unrestricted world patches. Current due actions run after clock advancement and before `campaign.before-day` and cadence systems. Hook-created Triggers wait until a later completed day. Stable Mod-namespaced IDs deduplicate identical pending/recent definitions; conflicting reuse fails. Recent receipts are bounded to 256.
- Thresholds, tags, workforce categories, effects and settlement definitions remain Mod-defined INI content.

11. SYSTEM EVENT, DOMAIN EVENT AND PRESENTATION BOUNDARY
------------------------------------------------------
- **System Event** is an internal processing request created when a persisted Trigger becomes due. It carries a stable type, Trigger/source IDs, campaign timestamp and JSON-safe payload; a registered Engine Resolution handles it. It is transient and is not maintained as a second permanent event history.
- **Domain Event** is a completed fact recorded in the authoritative world history (for example, `trigger.resolved`). A pending Domain Event Trigger may match a newly recorded fact inside a candidate world, capture its source snapshot and wait until the next completed day. Both daily simulation settlement (`engine/simulation.cjs`) and command transactions (`engine/command-runtime.cjs`) activate matching pending Triggers when they append a new fact. The read-only Event Bus still publishes only after the world commit and is not a gameplay executor.
- The two event types are not interchangeable. The Event Bus does not execute gameplay actions or mutate world state.
- Player-facing descriptions, icons, notifications, reports and popups belong to Presentation. A System Event does not itself represent text shown to the player.
- Engine Resolution handlers return a JSON-safe `{ result, effects, domainEvents? }` plan from a detached world snapshot and do not mutate authoritative World state or write Domain Event history. Effects and optional Domain Event descriptors are validated separately and each capped at 16. The shared `engine/settlement.cjs` runner applies ordered Effects through the existing validated Mod Effect handler and returns applied outcomes plus Domain Event descriptors; it does not own Trigger scheduling or event-history writes. Trigger Runner appends approved planned Domain Events inside the candidate-world transaction before recording `trigger.resolved`.
- The current Resolution consumers are `effect.apply`, `formula.effect` and `event.emit`. The latter emits only a Mod-namespaced Domain Event using the Trigger owner's identity; Mod supplies only a local event type suffix and object payload, not scope/subject/source identity, idempotency key, arbitrary callback or Event Bus access. A new fact can activate another pending Trigger for the next completed day. `formula.effect` only invokes an existing Engine-registered versioned formula; it captures JSON-safe inputs when scheduled, uses a stable random stream derived from the Trigger ID and maps top-level result fields into approved Effect payload fields. It does not provide Mod-defined formulas or an expression language.
- The Trigger Queue owns only due detection, System Event creation, completion facts, removal and receipts; it calls the reusable Settlement runner rather than implementing resolution/effect execution itself. A successful Trigger records the calculation result and applied Effect results in `trigger.resolved`; each Effect also uses the standard `mod.effect-applied` fact. Calculation audit, random state, effects, queue removal, Domain Events and receipts commit together with the candidate day.
- Domain Event source snapshots preserve the full validated JSON-safe source payload; the 16 KiB Trigger-definition payload limit must not be reused for a matched Domain Event snapshot. Daily settlement validates the Trigger Queue once in `processDueTriggers()`; save/world validation still checks it at the persistence boundary.

- Electron storage accepts schema 1–9 for reads and only schema 9 for new writes; main-process loading migrates legacy saves before activation.

12. POPULATION LOCATION, AFFILIATION, OWNERSHIP AND MOVEMENT — DESIGN GUARDRAILS
---------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Population remains aggregate/group-based. A Pop group is an authoritative population-count record, not a duplicated record copied into faction, colony and building containers.
- The intended conceptual hierarchy is political/organizational context → colony as current residence/location → population group as the authoritative aggregate. Faction summaries are derived views, not another population ledger.
- Buildings are workplaces. Actual employment must be represented as a relationship/allocation referencing resident population groups and headcounts; employed people remain part of colony population and must not be counted a second time or disappear from resident needs.
- Keep distinct concepts separate: current location/residence, political jurisdiction or affiliation, origin/homeland/manufacturing origin, employment, and (where applicable) legal ownership/control. A single generic owner/faction field must not stand in for all of them.
- Origin and ownership are optional semantics, not mandatory attributes for every organic population group. For synthetic/android/mechanical populations, manufacturing origin and ownership may matter; ownership can differ from political jurisdiction, current location and employer.
- Migration, transfer, natural population change, manufacturing/creation, attrition/scrapping, employment change, political control change and ownership transfer are distinct operations. Migration changes location; unemployment changes employment; jurisdiction changes political control; ownership transfer does not by itself move or create/destroy population.
- Cross-colony movement must conserve the transferred headcount globally and update source/destination authoritative records as one atomic operation. Do not implement this until the group identity, split/merge and transaction semantics are designed against actual consumers.
- Do not introduce individual records for ordinary residents. Named personnel remain a separate narrative/strategic pool and any abstraction from population must define whether the source group count changes to prevent double counting.
- These are durable design constraints, not an approved immediate schema or implementation. Defer migration, ownership registries and origin-distribution mechanics until a concrete consumer requires them.

13. SCI-FI POPULATION AND ECONOMY PREMISES
------------------------------------------
- The world includes organic Pops, synthetic/android Pops and mechanical-labor Pops. Population type/need profile must be capable of distinguishing their maintenance needs: organic populations need food and other biological necessities; synthetic/mechanical populations do not need food and instead require energy and precision components/maintenance.
- AI and mechanical labor reduce the assumption that production capacity is proportional to organic headcount. Production must eventually distinguish throughput/capacity, workforce requirement, energy input and maintenance.
- Energy is a central strategic/economic bottleneck, not merely an unlimited ordinary market input. Stellar generation and technological generation have location- and capacity-dependent limits; colonies and star systems compete for energy supply.
- The setting supports interstellar travel and gates, including mixed transport modes; colonization is possible on inhospitable worlds. Food can be cultivated locally, but self-sufficiency in unsuitable environments is extremely expensive.
- These premises guide future generic Engine capability design. Specific species, commodities, need profiles, energy technologies, costs and balance remain Mod INI content. Do not add these concrete definitions to Engine.
- Future energy design must distinguish generation capacity/available power, actual consumption and allocation, storage, and transportable energy carriers. Do not assume all forms of energy are interchangeable as ordinary colony stockpile quantities before the domain contract is decided.


14. CLASS-BASED NEEDS, PRODUCT QUALITY AND MARKET GRADES
-------------------------------------------------------
- Preserve the Mod-defined species population dimension for human/alien compatibility. Engine treats all populations as generic Pop Groups; it does not hardcode separate runtime entity classes for organic, android, alien or mechanical populations.
- Species, profession, class/social status, wealth and legal status are distinct concepts. Slavery is not a species. Do not automatically equate wealth_tier with class. Mod definitions decide categories and mappings.
- User's confirmed direction for needs: class/social level affects acceptable and expected quality/grade of need goods. Quantity consumption can be met while quality/lifestyle expectations remain unmet; report these separately.
- Goods may carry multiple Mod-defined tags (e.g. carbohydrate and protein) with different quality contributions. Production/building/process modifiers can raise or lower quality; negative processing labels may reduce it. Exact formula, weights and tag semantics remain open.
- Market grade is contextual: compare competing goods for the same need category in a specific market. It is not an immutable commodity property. Market benchmark weighting and absolute-vs-relative thresholds remain open.
- A Need Profile must have explicit population assignment. Never infer a class × species or other joint population by multiplying independent marginal dimensions. An explicit need_profile dimension is a candidate for representing exact need-cohort shares, pending final contract design.
- If production can create different quality batches under the same commodity ID, a quantity-only stockpile loses quality provenance. Future inventory design must explicitly preserve variants/batches or choose another clear representation; do not implement it in the current design phase.
- Engine mechanisms remain generic; Need Profiles, class standards, tags, product mappings, modifiers and values belong to Mod INI.


14. TAG-BASED POPULATION NEED DESIGN (2026-11-23)
-------------------------------------------------
- User decision: use Tags as the unified descriptive/matching vocabulary; do not create a special NeedProfile runtime entity or dedicated NeedProfile content type merely to classify Pops.
- Species remains a Mod-defined population dimension/category, compatible with humans, aliens and future intelligent life. Mechanical laborers and slaves are not automatically separate species; synthetic and organic populations remain generic Pop Groups in Engine.

A Tag rule that needs a joint cohort must use explicitly represented population membership/shares or a directly classified cohort; never multiply independent dimension proportions to invent a joint count.
- The exact INI representation and matching operators remain to be designed. Reuse existing population-group dimension distributions where they express explicit population shares; do not add a broad Tag framework or new runtime layer without a concrete consumer.
